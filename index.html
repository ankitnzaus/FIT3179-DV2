AUSTRALIA'S FOOD SYSTEM

From Farm to Fork

How Australia's food system begins, moves and reaches our tables

Australia produces a diverse range of food across vastly different landscapes. This visualisation explores where food is produced, what different states specialise in, how the system connects to markets, how prices reach households, and what happens when food is ultimately wasted.

01

Where Australia's food begins

Australia's horticultural production is unevenly distributed across the country, with a small number of states accounting for much of the value generated by fruit, vegetables, nuts and wine grapes.

02

What Australia produces

Australia's food production varies significantly across states. Wheat production is concentrated in major broadacre regions, while horticultural industries show distinct state-level specialisations.

03

From farm to market

Australia's agricultural industries are closely connected to overseas markets. In 2024–25, a relatively small group of countries accounted for a large share of Australia's agriculture, fisheries and forestry export value.

China was Australia's largest AFF export destination in 2024–25 at about A$17.0 billion — almost twice the value exported to the United States.

China remained Australia's largest AFF export market throughout the period, while the United States moved from third to second place. India recorded the sharpest rise among the highlighted destinations.

04

The price of food

What happens on farms and in markets eventually reaches household budgets. Food prices continued to rise through 2025 and 2026, although their pace did not always match the broader inflation rate.

Food and non-alcoholic beverage prices were 3.3% higher in June 2026 than a year earlier, compared with 3.8% inflation across the overall CPI.

Across the eight capital cities, food inflation was generally below overall CPI in June 2026. Adelaide showed one of the widest gaps, with food prices rising 3.1% compared with 4.2% headline inflation.

05

What happens at the end?

Not all food produced and purchased is eventually eaten. Australia wastes around 7.6 million tonnes of food each year, with losses occurring from farms and factories all the way through to restaurants and households.

Households are the largest single source in the national baseline, generating about 2.5 million tonnes of food waste each year.

Put another way, roughly three out of every ten tonnes of Australia's food waste comes from households alone.

Modelling for the national strategy estimated that food waste could reach 9.2 million tonnes annually by 2030 without further action. Preventing around 4.8 million tonnes would reduce that to about 4.4 million tonnes under the recommended scenario.
